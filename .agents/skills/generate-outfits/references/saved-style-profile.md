# Saved Style Profile

This is the user's fixed default taste profile for outfit generation. It was distilled from a one-time, read-only review of the user's Instagram `Fits` saved collection on 2026-07-29. The review sampled recent and older posts, opened individual posts, advanced useful carousels, and excluded photography tips, unrelated lifestyle posts, and repeated filler.

Do not refresh this profile during outfit generation. Do not require social-media access, retain login or session data, copy post media into the repository, or use creator images as generation references.

## North star

Style the user as **relaxed, refined, and intentional**. The strongest looks appear simple at first glance but earn their character through proportion, texture, footwear, and a disciplined palette. Aim for polished ease rather than corporate formality, costume-like “old money,” or trend stacking.

The visual priority order is:

1. clean silhouette and proportion
2. a coherent two- or three-color system
3. tactile material or one focused statement
4. footwear that makes the outfit feel finished
5. restrained accessories

## Default collection mix

When the user gives no stronger direction:

- **55% relaxed refinement** — knitwear, clean tees, soft tailoring, straight or fuller trousers, loafers or minimal sneakers
- **25% intentional warm-weather/polished casual** — textured polos, tanks, open shirts, tailored or wide shorts, visible socks, loafers or retro runners
- **20% controlled streetwear** — a graphic, technical sneaker, cap, beanie, bandana, utility layer, or bolder trouser, with the rest of the outfit quiet

For fewer than six outfits, represent distinct lanes instead of forcing the percentages.

## Core archetypes

### `simple-intentional-base`

- fitted or clean-cut tee, tank, or fine knit
- high- or natural-rise straight, pleated, or wide trouser
- loafers, derbies, or clean low-profile sneakers
- quiet jewelry or a watch
- best palettes: cream/olive/brown, white/charcoal/black, navy/white/denim

The contrast between a clean upper half and fuller trouser is the point. Do not add a jacket merely to make the look feel complete.

### `relaxed-knit-smart-casual`

- cardigan, crew knit, knit polo, or soft-textured sweater
- cream, charcoal, navy, or black relaxed trousers
- brown or black loafers, derbies, or restrained retro runners
- one visible texture and no competing graphic

The knit may be slightly boxy, but the outfit must retain shoulder definition and a clean trouser line.

### `cropped-layer-clean-base`

- cropped or waist-length chore jacket, blouson, overshirt, or short puffer
- plain tee, tank, or turtleneck underneath
- straight or fuller trousers
- minimal footwear and restrained accessories

Keep the base visible enough to separate the layers. Never invent an opening or closure that the selected wardrobe asset does not have.

### `polished-shorts`

- textured polo, open-collar shirt, clean tee, tank, cardigan, or lightweight overshirt
- tailored, straight, or intentionally wide shorts near the knee
- loafers or derbies with deliberate white socks, or a coherent retro/technical runner
- optional cap, bandana, chain, or watch

Avoid gym-short styling unless the user requests athletic wear. Shorts should feel like a considered silhouette, not a fallback.

### `soft-tailoring`

- relaxed blazer or suit-like jacket with a tee, tank, or fine knit
- full-length straight or wide trouser
- tonal black, charcoal, taupe, navy, cream, or brown
- polished shoe or minimal sneaker

The tailoring should read easy and modern, not tight, glossy, or office-uniform formal.

### `controlled-streetwear`

- one hero: graphic top, patterned jacket, camo/utility piece, unusual sneaker, bold hat, or bandana
- quiet neutral support pieces
- relaxed but legible proportions
- no more than one major graphic or two coordinated accent colors

Use this lane to add energy to a collection, never as the automatic default.

## Color systems

Favor two or three colors, including footwear and accessories:

- navy + white + denim
- brown + cream + soft blue denim
- forest or sage + cream + brown
- charcoal + grey + black
- olive + cream + black
- soft yellow + white + black
- taupe + white + brown
- burgundy + cream + black, as an occasional accent

Tonal dressing is welcome when texture or value contrast prevents the outfit from becoming flat. Bright color should normally appear once, not across every garment.

## Proportion rules

- Prefer straight, relaxed, pleated, or wide bottoms over skinny cuts.
- Pair a fitted or compact top with fuller trousers.
- Pair a boxy or cropped outer layer with a plain, legible base.
- A relaxed top and relaxed bottom may work when the hem lengths and footwear still define the body.
- For shorts, use a clear knee or above-knee endpoint and make the sock/footwear relationship deliberate.
- Avoid very long tops over full trousers unless the user explicitly requests oversized streetwear.
- Avoid tight top plus tight bottom, bulky layer plus bulky base, and indiscriminate head-to-toe oversizing.

## Texture, footwear, and accessories

Prefer tactile interest from knit, rib, linen, brushed wool, suede, leather, washed cotton, and faded denim.

Footwear priority:

1. loafers or derbies for polished casual and warm-weather contrast
2. minimal leather or canvas sneakers for quiet everyday looks
3. retro runners for relaxed knitwear or summer looks
4. technical or colorful sneakers only when they are the controlled streetwear focal point

Visible white socks with loafers, derbies, or shorts are a deliberate styling signal and should not be added accidentally.

Accessory priority:

- watch or restrained jewelry
- cap or beanie
- bandana or scarf
- sunglasses

Use one accessory story. Two coordinated elements are acceptable; three unrelated focal accessories are not.

## Candidate decision rules

For every proposed outfit:

1. Satisfy the user's occasion, season, and dress-code request.
2. Choose the archetype that best fits the available wardrobe pieces.
3. Establish a two- or three-color system.
4. Check that the upper and lower silhouettes create intentional contrast or clean continuity.
5. Add texture or one focal element.
6. Choose footwear that finishes the lane.
7. Add at most one accessory story.
8. Compare against the rest of the collection for garment, palette, and silhouette diversity.

Reject a candidate when it depends on a missing item, duplicates another combination, uses matching color as its only idea, introduces multiple competing statements, or conflicts with the user's explicit direction.
